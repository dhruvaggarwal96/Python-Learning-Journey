# 🧠 Model Selection Thinking

For a Customer Churn Prediction problem, I should be able to reason through:

1. What model would I start with?
2. Why would I choose that model?
3. What metric should I prioritize?
4. What should I do if the dataset is imbalanced?
5. How should I compare different models?
6. How can I avoid overfitting?
7. How would I explain my final model to a non-technical manager?

### Important principle

> **There is no universally best classification model.**

Model selection depends on:

* Dataset characteristics
* Feature types
* Dataset size
* Interpretability requirements
* Computational resources
* Business objective
* Error costs
* Model performance

Revise the four outcomes of a binary classification problem.

| Actual   | Predicted | Result         |
| -------- | --------- | -------------- |
| Positive | Positive  | True Positive  |
| Negative | Negative  | True Negative  |
| Negative | Positive  | False Positive |
| Positive | Negative  | False Negative |

---

# 📞 Customer Churn Example

Suppose:

> **Positive = Customer will churn**

Then:

### True Positive

The model predicts:

> "This customer will churn."

And the customer actually churns.

### True Negative

The model predicts:

> "This customer will not churn."

And the customer actually stays.

### False Positive

The model predicts:

> "This customer will churn."

But the customer actually stays.

### False Negative

The model predicts:

> "This customer will not churn."

But the customer actually churns.

---

# 💼 Business Thinking

For churn prediction, I should not evaluate a model using accuracy alone.

I need to ask:

> **What is the business cost of each type of mistake?**

For example:

* False Positive → company may waste retention resources on a customer who would have stayed.
* False Negative → company may fail to identify a customer who was actually going to leave.

The correct priority depends on the company's business objective and the relative cost of each error.
